About

This is my community.

Gomoa Fetteh, my family's hometown, is a fishing community on the coast of Ghana's Central Region. Its pre-kindergarten class met in a room with worn tables, few books, and no meal program.

The local church has taught children here for years with very little. Bright Start doesn't replace that work; it strengthens it with learning materials, daily meals, and support for the teacher.

— George Armah, Founder & Director

Why it costs more per child

Phase 2 works out to $160 per child, compared with about $95 in Phase 1. Food, furniture, and transport prices in Ghana have risen since Phase 1, a smaller order loses bulk pricing, and Phase 1 already covered one-time costs like teacher training and core books. Across both phases: $6,063 for 50 children, about $121 each.

What happens once it's funded

  1. Buy the furniture and materials in Ghana
  2. Welcome the 20 new children to class
  3. Run three months of daily meals and lessons
  4. Send every donor a final impact report with photos

Community

A fishing town on the coast of Ghana's Central Region.

Most days start at the shore, where canoes come in and families sort the catch. These are the families of the children we serve.

Looking ahead

The community is building permanent classrooms.

Families in Gomoa Fetteh have started construction on a permanent school building with concrete-block walls. Finishing it will take an estimated $70,000.

Today's pre-kindergarten children will be in primary school before it opens, so Bright Start focuses on what they need right now.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

How is my donation used?

Donations pay for program costs in Gomoa Fetteh: furniture, learning materials, daily meals, and teacher support. The Phase 2 budget above shows every line. We document purchases with photos and share them with donors.

Is my donation tax-deductible?

No. Bright Start Ghana Initiative is not currently recognized as a 501(c)(3) tax-exempt organization, so donations are not tax-deductible at this time. We send an acknowledgment of every gift for your records.

How do I know the money reaches the children?

George Armah, the founder, grew up in Gomoa Fetteh and works directly with family and church contacts there. Donors receive photos of materials delivered and progress updates from the classroom.

Will I hear how it went?

Yes. You'll get a thank-you and acknowledgment right away, photo updates while the program runs, and a final impact report at the end of each phase.

Can I visit?

Yes. If you're traveling to Ghana, we can arrange a visit to Gomoa Fetteh. We can also set up a video call with the classroom.
