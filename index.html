Why this matters

A classroom full of potential — and almost no resources.

In Gomoa Fetteh, pre-kindergarten children attend class without books, proper materials, or consistent meals. Their teacher does remarkable work with very little. We're changing that — not by starting something new, but by strengthening what the community already built.

The need

Children learning without books, supplies, or dependable nutrition — and families struggling to fill the gap.

Our approach

Learning materials, a daily feeding program, teacher support, and parent engagement — a whole-child foundation.

The impact

Every child has what they need to learn. Meals remove the hunger barrier. The groundwork lasts well beyond the first term.

The school's head teacher in the classroom on launch day, March 4, 2026.

Why Gomoa Fetteh, why now

This is my community.

Gomoa Fetteh is my family's hometown. I've seen firsthand the challenges these children face — and the resilience of the people around them. This isn't a distant charity project; it's about lifting up my own community's youngest generation.

The local church has taught children here for years with almost nothing — they have the infrastructure, the commitment, and the trust of families. Pre-kindergarten is when the foundation for all future learning is set. Investing now means these children enter primary school ready to succeed.

— George Armah, Founder & Director

The community

Meet Gomoa Fetteh.

The children, the classroom, and the village your support reaches.

Life in Gomoa Fetteh

A fishing town on Ghana's Central Region coast.

Most days start at the shore, where canoes come in and families sort the catch. These are the parents and grandparents of the children we serve.

The bigger picture

Building for the decades ahead.

While we focus on the 50 children learning today, the community is constructing permanent classrooms — a major, locally driven investment that will serve hundreds of children for years to come.

But children can't wait for construction to finish. Today's pre-KG students will be in primary school before the new building opens. Both paths matter: the community builds for the future, and Bright Start provides what children need right now.

Transparency

Questions, answered.

How will my donation be used?

Every dollar goes directly to the children — learning materials, the feeding program, teacher support, and parent engagement. We document purchases with photos and share them with donors.

Is my donation tax-deductible?

We're working toward 501(c)(3) status. For now, donations are not yet tax-deductible, but we provide receipts for your records, and we expect tax-exempt status in the near future.

How do I know the money reaches the children?

Accountability is our priority. George Armah (founder) has direct contacts in Gomoa Fetteh through family and church networks. Donors receive photos of materials delivered, teacher progress reports, local church oversight, and regular updates.

What happens after the program term?

This is a sustainable launch, not a one-time handout. Materials last for years, teacher training is permanent, and parent workshops shift mindsets. The community takes ownership — the goal is to empower, not create dependency.

Can I sponsor a specific child?

We currently support the whole classroom as a group so no child is left out. Larger donors ($500+) can be matched with specific classroom needs and receive detailed updates.

Will I receive updates?

Yes — a thank-you and receipt right away, monthly progress updates with photos, and a final impact report after the program term.
